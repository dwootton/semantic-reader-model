# Small semantic-interface model: evidence and feasibility

Research note, checked 2026-09-12. Published findings are distinguished from proposed experiments. No model was trained or benchmarked for this note.

## What the evidence supports

A small local model for **bounded, source-grounded grouping and relation prediction for human assistive navigation** is a plausible research project. Existing work supports the ingredients—UI representations, synthetic supervision, compact UI models, and hierarchy prediction—but does not establish that a 0.5B–3B model can produce a universally correct, useful simplified hierarchy across arbitrary web and native applications. The development machine is a Mac, while the observation format should preserve both AXDOM and UIA source evidence.

The strongest near-term outcome is a reproducible corpus and benchmark, an explicit annotation policy, and evidence comparing deterministic rules, a compact relation model, and fine-tuned language models. A gold-standard cross-platform accessibility product requires more than a successful training run.

Raw HTML and screenshots are available; its task is conversational web navigation. ## Model choices that actually fit the question

Current official model cards offer more relevant options than only older Gemma/Qwen releases:

| Candidate | Official facts | Experiment role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Qwen3.5-0.8B](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-0.8B) | Language model with a vision encoder; the language component is 0.8B. The model page reports approximately 0.9B total parameters. The card explicitly identifies task-specific fine-tuning and prototyping as intended uses and warns about thinking loops at this scale. | Fast text-only grouping student and an optional same-family screenshot ablation. Disable reasoning output for the production experiment; predict only required structure. |
| [Qwen3.5-2B](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-2B) | 2B language component plus vision encoder, native 262,144-token context, hybrid linear/full attention. The card provides a language-model-only serving option. | Larger paired student to establish whether 0.8B is the quality bottleneck. Advertised context capacity is not evidence of good long-tree reasoning or acceptable laptop latency. |
| [Qwen3-0.6B](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-0.6B) | Text-only causal language model, 0.6B parameters, 32,768-token context. | Useful conventional text baseline if its runtime or training stack is easier to stabilize in the sprint. |
| [Gemma 4 E2B](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/core/model_card_4) | Text/image/audio support and 128K context. It is 2.3B **effective**, but 5.1B with embeddings; the model card separately lists approximately 150M vision and 300M audio encoders. | A worthwhile device-focused comparison if the requirement concerns measured memory and latency. It does not meet a strict 3B total-weight definition. Do not equate effective/active counts with downloaded weights or RAM. |

These are candidates, not a ranking for this untested task. Start with 0.8B and 2B from the same family to isolate model-size effects, plus a rules baseline and a compact node-pair/group scorer. A structured classifier may be faster than autoregressively rewriting every tree node. The recent contextual-association study supplies a concrete precedent for testing MLP/graph approaches.

[MLX-LM](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-lm/blob/main/mlx_lm/LORA.md) documents LoRA, QLoRA, and full fine-tuning paths. [llama.cpp](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp) supports local quantized inference and grammar-constrained output. Exact checkpoint/backend support must be smoke-tested rather than assumed. Train on available accelerators; separately evaluate the exported artifact on the target laptop.

For a conventional dense model, raw four-bit weights require roughly `parameters × 0.5 bytes`: 0.25 GB for 0.5B and 1.5 GB for 3B, in decimal units. This is arithmetic, **not total runtime memory**: scales, embeddings retained at another precision, encoders, caches, activations, and the runtime add overhead. Quantize only after establishing an unquantized quality baseline, then rerun the same benchmark.

## What changes the approach

**Predict edits or relations, not a copy of the whole tree.** Keep source nodes and their authoritative labels/states in the captured IR. Have the learner emit group membership, parent/group relations, short inferred labels, reading-order adjustments, and uncertainty. Reconstruct the user view deterministically. This reduces generation length and allows references, action preservation, and acyclicity to be checked in code. A group label is inferred; a checkbox's checked state is observed.

**A tree may be a projection of a richer graph.** A price belongs with its product card; its currency may also relate to a global selector. An error explains a field while appearing elsewhere in the DOM. Preserve relations separately from the hierarchy chosen for navigation. Otherwise the annotation policy can force annotators into arbitrary single-parent choices.

**Capture screenshots now, decide whether students need them by ablation.** Compare AX-only, AX plus geometry/layout, and AX plus screenshot on the same examples. If a teacher uses information visible only in an image, a text-only student cannot reliably recover that information from an impoverished input. Better teacher reasoning cannot fix this information mismatch. Provide the relevant evidence at inference time or constrain labels to what the student can observe.

**Do not equate model consensus with gold.** [LLM-as-a-judge research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685) documents position, verbosity, and self-enhancement biases. Multiple expensive judges may share the same interpretation. Use synthetic annotations as scalable training data; reserve “gold” for an independently adjudicated evaluation core, with stated user goals and acceptable alternatives. Include screen-reader users if the product claim concerns their navigation experience.

**Split by site, application, and template family before generation.** Random screenshot splits can put near-identical menus and product templates on both sides. Test novel sites/apps, missing labels, repeated cards, dense tables, dialogs, editable rich text, tree views, localization, and loading/error/empty states. Preserve state transitions to test whether group IDs and reading order remain stable when the UI changes.

**Evaluate utility under preservation constraints.** Report source-reference validity, action/state preservation, incorrect merges, missed grouping relations, ordering quality, and downstream navigation/task performance. Compare pairwise memberships or relations when multiple equivalent hierarchy shapes are acceptable. A shorter output or high judge preference can conceal an omitted control. Schema validation checks syntax, not whether the grouping is useful. For a human assistive tool, evaluate orientation, time to find a control, task success, recovery from incorrect grouping, and whether updates disrupt the user's current location.

**Treat the timing requirement as a pipeline requirement.** Under one second is an aspirational target; up to ten seconds is the acceptable initial range. For the subsecond lane, test deterministic layout/group rules plus a compact encoder/ranker and incremental updates. For the initial language-model lane, generate short source-ID relations or group edits, then reconstruct and render the view in code. Keep the model loaded during warm-latency measurements and report cold startup separately. Avoid promising either threshold from parameter count alone; no exact laptop configuration has been specified.

## A defensible 72-hour experiment

This schedule is a proposal, not a promise of throughput or dataset quality:

1. **First 8 hours:** define one reader task and grouping policy; collect a deliberately varied pilot; annotate enough examples independently to expose ambiguous cases. Freeze a small evaluation set before optimizing the teacher prompt. Record input/output tokens and run the target laptop baseline immediately.
2. **Hours 8–24:** finalize the common observation fields and lossless source references; test deterministic simplification and a strong teacher on the pilot. Add hard validators and dispute reasons. Generate a modest synthetic tranche while manually reviewing a stratified sample.
3. **Hours 24–48:** fine-tune the 0.8B and 2B candidates; compare them with rules and compact relation scoring. Run text/layout/vision ablations. Expand synthetic labeling only where the first results show a learnable, consistent target.
4. **Hours 48–72:** evaluate held-out domains and transitions, quantize/export, measure target-device latency and memory, and manually inspect failures. Package captures, annotation policy, split manifest, teacher provenance, benchmark, and model checkpoint. Report which claims the evidence supports.

Measure capture time, prefill latency, generated tokens, decode latency, p50/p95 end-to-end latency, peak memory, and repeated-update stability. Parameter count and tokens/second alone are insufficient. A small model generating thousands of JSON tokens can feel slow; compact IDs, short outputs, bounded subtrees, and incremental updates may matter more than reducing another few hundred million parameters.

Unlimited compute can buy more candidates, more diverse captures, and more training experiments. It cannot recover information absent from the capture, decide whose preferred navigation structure defines correctness, recruit and calibrate expert annotators instantly, or make correlated labels independent. The first bottleneck to resolve is the target definition and evidence sufficiency; only then is large-scale judging worth accelerating.
